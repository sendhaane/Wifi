1. Key features of Wi-Fi 6, 6E, and 7 and how they differ from Wi-Fi 5

Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac) focused mainly on higher speeds using wider channels (up to 160 MHz) and MU-MIMO (downlink only).

Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) – Improvements over Wi-Fi 5
Works in 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz
Introduces OFDMA (multiple users transmit simultaneously)
Supports uplink and downlink MU-MIMO
Uses 1024-QAM for higher data rates
Includes Target Wake Time (TWT) for power saving
Better performance in dense environments

Wi-Fi 6E – Extension of Wi-Fi 6
Adds 6 GHz band (in addition to 2.4 and 5 GHz)
Provides more channels and less interference
Supports wider channels (up to 160 MHz)
Lower congestion compared to older bands

Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) – Next generation
Works in 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz
Supports 320 MHz channel width
Uses 4096-QAM (higher data rate)
Introduces Multi-Link Operation (MLO)
Improved OFDMA and MU-MIMO
Much higher throughput (up to ~46 Gbps)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Role of OFDMA in Wi-Fi 6 and how it improves efficiency

OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access) is a key feature of Wi-Fi 6 that improves how multiple devices share the channel.

Role of OFDMA
Splits a Wi-Fi channel into smaller units called Resource Units (RUs)
Assigns different RUs to different devices
Allows multiple devices to transmit simultaneously
How it improves efficiency
Reduces waiting time
Multiple devices can send data at the same time instead of taking turns
Better for small data packets
Efficiently handles traffic like IoT, notifications, and small uploads
Improves performance in dense networks
Works well in crowded environments like offices and campuses
Lower latency
Faster access to the channel reduces delays
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Benefits of Target Wake Time (TWT) in Wi-Fi 6 for IoT devices

Target Wake Time (TWT) is a power-saving feature in Wi-Fi 6 where the AP schedules when a device should wake up to transmit or receive data.

Benefits
Improved battery life
Devices sleep for longer periods and wake only at scheduled times, reducing power consumption.
Reduced network contention
Since devices wake at different times, fewer devices compete for the channel simultaneously.
Efficient communication for IoT
Ideal for sensors and smart devices that send small, periodic data.
Predictable communication
Scheduled wake times ensure consistent and reliable data transmission.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Significance of the 6 GHz frequency band in Wi-Fi 6E

The 6 GHz band is an extension introduced in Wi-Fi 6E to overcome congestion in older Wi-Fi bands.

Significance
More available spectrum
The 6 GHz band provides a large number of new channels, reducing congestion.
Less interference
Unlike 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, the 6 GHz band is relatively unused, leading to cleaner signals.
Wider channels
Supports wider channels (up to 160 MHz), enabling higher data rates.
Better performance in dense environments
Ideal for places with many devices like offices and campuses.
Supports modern devices only
Since only newer devices use this band, there is less legacy interference.
------------------------------------------------------------
5. Comparison between Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E (range, bandwidth, interference)

Wi-Fi 6 operates in 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, while Wi-Fi 6E extends Wi-Fi 6 by adding the 6 GHz band.

Range
Wi-Fi 6 (2.4 GHz / 5 GHz) → Better range, especially in 2.4 GHz
Wi-Fi 6E (6 GHz) → Shorter range due to higher frequency
Bandwidth
Wi-Fi 6 → Limited by crowded 2.4 and 5 GHz bands
Wi-Fi 6E → Much higher available bandwidth due to new 6 GHz spectrum
Interference
Wi-Fi 6 → More interference from legacy devices
Wi-Fi 6E → Very low interference since only newer devices use 6 GHz
-----------------------------------------------------------
6. Major innovations introduced in Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be)

Wi-Fi 7 introduces several advancements to achieve very high speed, low latency, and improved efficiency.

Key Innovations
320 MHz Channel Width
Doubles the channel width compared to Wi-Fi 6, enabling much higher data rates.
4096-QAM Modulation
Increases the number of bits per symbol, improving throughput.
Multi-Link Operation (MLO)
Allows devices to use multiple frequency bands simultaneously for better performance.
Enhanced OFDMA
Improves resource allocation and reduces latency.
Multi-RU Allocation
A single user can use multiple resource units, increasing efficiency.
Higher MIMO Capability
Supports more spatial streams, improving capacity and speed.

8. Purpose of 802.11k and 802.11v and how they aid in roaming

802.11k and 802.11v are standards that help improve roaming decisions in Wi-Fi networks.

802.11k (Radio Resource Management)
Provides information about nearby APs
The current AP shares a neighbor report with the client
Helps the client quickly identify better APs

Benefit:
Reduces scanning time and speeds up roaming

802.11v (Network Management)
Allows the network to guide the client to a better AP
Uses BSS Transition Management messages
Helps in load balancing and better AP selection

Benefit:
Improves decision-making for roaming
-------------------------------------------------------
9. Fast BSS Transition (802.11r) and its benefit in mobile environments

802.11r (Fast BSS Transition) is a standard that enables fast and seamless roaming between Access Points.

Concept

Normally, when a device moves to a new AP:

It must perform full authentication again
This causes delay

With 802.11r:

Authentication keys are pre-shared or derived in advance
Device can quickly switch to a new AP
----------------------------------------------------
10. How 802.11k, 802.11v, and 802.11r work together for seamless roaming

These three standards work together to provide fast and efficient roaming in enterprise Wi-Fi networks.

Roles of Each Standard
802.11k
Provides a list of nearby APs (neighbor report)
→ Helps the client know where to roam
802.11v
Suggests the best AP to connect to
→ Guides the client’s decision
802.11r
Enables fast authentication during roaming
→ Reduces handoff delay
